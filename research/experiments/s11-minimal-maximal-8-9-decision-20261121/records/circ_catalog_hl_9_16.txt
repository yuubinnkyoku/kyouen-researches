  Hence (finite exhaustive verification): at n=5..8 every circle with >=5 lattice points has a
  half-lattice center; at n=9..12 every circle with >=7 points does. Non-half-lattice circles
  (guaranteed, = census minus wide-hl, attributed exactly because wide=census for classes>=5 and
  the counts are range-stable per the n=10 spot check):
    n=9:  5-pt 32,  6-pt 4          n=10: 5-pt 96,  6-pt 28
    n=11: 5-pt 152, 6-pt 88         n=12: 5-pt 236, 6-pt 180
  Class 4 is not attributed (hl count keeps growing with scan range, e.g. n=10: 4085 -> 4152 from
  [ -2n,4n ) to [ -4n,8n ); census n=10 4-class = 10868).
- Parity/evenness remark (standard two-squares theory, no novelty claimed): points on a circle with
  center in (1/2)Z^2 pair up under (x,y) -> (i-x, j-y) with no fixed point, so the FULL-plane count is
  always even; odd grid classes (9, 11) are window truncations. Full-plane count equals r2(d2) for
  d2 = 2 mod 4, r2(d2/4) for d2 = 0 mod 4, r2(d2)/2 for odd d2 (mixed-parity centers).

## 4. Scope and caveats
- All catalogs are half-lattice-center catalogs. Full-rational-center census exists only for n<=12,
  where classes >=7 are entirely half-lattice; the n=13..20 counts could in principle miss non-half-lattice
  circles (none exist with >=7 points for n<=12; no claim for n>=13).
- In-range completeness for classes >=9 verified by wide scan at n=13..16 (n=17..20 not wide-checked).
- Family "laws" above are finite observations over their stated ranges; window-slack derivations exist
  in principle (finite translation windows of full-plane rep sets) but are not claimed as theorems here.
